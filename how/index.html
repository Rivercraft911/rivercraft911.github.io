One beam

How the intro works, and why nothing in it is fake · River Dowdy

Ok so that minute of chaos before my projects? All of it is drawn by one simulated oscilloscope beam. No video, no sprites, no images. Just a single dot of light flying around a phosphor screen a couple million times a second, and wherever it has been the screen glows for a bit and then fades. Everything you just watched is wherever that dot went.

There was exactly one rule: nothing on screen is fake. If something claims to be a signal, it is one. The equations up top are live, drawn by the same beam, and the numbers ticking inside them are the numbers actually drawing the picture. And for the first half, the sound is the picture. Left speaker is the beam's x, right speaker is its y. So if you plugged a real scope into your headphone jack (please do this, it would make my week) it would draw the site.

The phosphor

A beam dragging across a tube dumps energy along its path. So every little step of the beam, from a to b, lays down a Gaussian spot smeared along that segment, which very kindly has a closed form, a difference of two error functions:

E(p) ∝ ∫01 e−|p − a − s(b−a)|² ⁄ 2σ² ds    E ← E · e−Δt⁄τ

The screen holds on to that energy and lets it decay exponentially, the way real phosphor does. A few percent of it scatters around inside the glass as a faint halo that falls off as r−2.5 (this is the part that makes it look like an actual tube and not a line drawing). Brightness is energy per second, so it looks the same at 60 Hz and 120 Hz. And if your display can do HDR, the core of the trace is brighter than white, which is super fun.

A square wave

It opens on the oldest trick in the book: a square wave is just a pile of sines, and you get to watch the pile get stacked.

x(t) = (4 ⁄ π) Σk sin((2k+1)ωt) ⁄ (2k+1)

Each harmonic shows up like a control loop locking. It overshoots, rings, and settles. Almost everything that moves in the intro moves this way, as a sum of second-order step responses, which has the very nice side effect that every frame is a pure function of time (so you can scrub it like a video):

s(τ) = 1 − e−ζωnτ (cos ωdτ + (ζωn ⁄ ωd) sin ωdτ)

That's why the corners flicker while they sharpen. Those are the Gibbs ears, and you can hear every harmonic land. All fifty-one of them arrive in about a second and a half, and the square is still ringing when the next part grabs it.

Ok so that's week one of Signals and Systems, and a reasonable person would stop here. I did not stop here.

The wave becomes a river

The square wave stops being a height and becomes a heading. Rivers turn as they run, and the curve real meandering rivers follow is the sine-generated curve of Langbein and Leopold (yes, my name is River, yes this was always going to happen):

θ(s) = ω sin(2πs ⁄ M)

Feed θ the square wave and you get a zigzag. Then the harmonics let go, highest first, and the elbows round off into meanders. The river's direction eiθ is literally phase modulation, so as the bends swell you can hear Bessel sidebands bloom. And the same Bessel function tells you how windy the river is, the length along the channel over the distance down the valley:

sinuosity = 1 ⁄ J0(ω)

That line lands exactly on the river in the valley. Not near it. On it.

The valley

The ground is a power-law random field, random phases under |k|−β, which is the same spectral trick behind basically all synthetic terrain, carved into a valley:

h(x) = 𝓕−1[ |k|−β eiφk ]

It's drawn the way the pulsar plot on Unknown Pleasures was drawn: cross-section after cross-section, each one hidden behind the ones in front of it, recomputed every single frame. The firs are triangle waves under a tapering envelope. The water is streamlines of an actual flow field, with a pool turning inside every bend.

The waterfall

If you've done any radio you have lost hours of your life staring at a waterfall plot: the spectrum of a signal, one row per moment, every new row shoving the old ones down the screen so the history pours away under you. Frequency across, time down, brightness is energy. It's called a waterfall because it looks like one. So at the end of the river there's a real one.

The camera flies to the lip, and the falling water stops pretending. Each lane of the curtain is a frequency bin. The lip is the live spectrum of exactly what you're hearing right now, and it tips over the edge row after row, so the curtain below is the recent past of the soundtrack, falling. It's the short-time Fourier transform of the audio, computed while it plays:

X(m, ω) = Σn x[n] w[n − mH] e−iωn

The camera pulls back until the falls are nothing but the plot. Then the plot pours into the pool below and winds itself around the drain: rows bend into rings, lanes fan out into angles, and the rectangle becomes a disc.

The fall

From here on the tube is a raster CRT too: full-screen fields, rendered on the GPU, swept into the same phosphor under the beam. (The beam keeps drawing on top, but from here it has help.) The falls land in a plunge pool that's already turning, a Burgers vortex,

vθ(r) = (Γ ⁄ 2πr) (1 − e−r² ⁄ rc²)

and the disc is still the waterfall plot, wound into a circle: angle is log-frequency and log-radius is age, so the water carries the spectrum's history inward. The soundtrack's octaves become its spiral arms.

Then text spreads over the whirlpool like an infection. Every cell turns into whichever teleprinter glyph has the ink that best matches the light behind it, and the centre row goes out as real RTTY: 45.45 baud, Baudot, 170 Hz shift. You could decode it. (Nobody will. It's there anyway.)

Down the drain is Indra's net: the limit set of a Kleinian group (Maskit's slice, straight out of Indra's Pearls), generated by just two Möbius maps,

a(z) = μ + 1 ⁄ z     b(z) = z + 2

and every pearl is a soap bubble lit by thin-film interference. On the floor the beam plays the chaos game, throwing random words in a, b and their inverses at a point, so the dust it leaves behind is the same limit set.

The multiverse

Then it cuts through ten worlds, faster and faster: a kaleidoscopic IFS, the Mandelbulb, an Apollonian packing, a Lyapunov fractal grown from the DVB-S2 start-of-frame word, Newton's method run on the roots of a 32APSK constellation, the {4,3,5} hyperbolic honeycomb, and more. Every world keeps running while you're somewhere else, and every world bends the music its own way. Last is a quaternion Julia set, a four-dimensional fractal, sliced by a plane that is turning through 4D (I cannot picture this either).

The choir

The music under the fall is a Shepard tone: octaves sliding down forever under a fixed bell-shaped loudness, so it always sounds like it's falling and it never gets anywhere:

x(t) = Σk A(log₂ fk) sin φk(t),    fk = 2k − D(t) f0

D(t) is how many octaves we've fallen and A is a fixed bell over log-frequency. The partials are pure octaves from 40 Hz to 5 kHz, sung in a little feedback-delay-network hall. The picture follows the same rule: every world is an endless zoom, and the zoom rate is the choir's rate of descent. You are falling exactly as fast as you hear yourself falling.

The black hole

At the end the Julia set falls into a black hole sitting in a pseudo-Kleinian cathedral. The light rays really are bent by the Schwarzschild metric on their way through the scene, which is why the arches wrap into Einstein rings around the shadow:

x″ = −3M h² x ⁄ r⁵

Then the cathedral itself gets torn in, spaghettified, and wound into a maelstrom by the shear of its own orbits. We fall with angular momentum 3.5M, just short of the 4M that would have let us escape, so we spiral in (so close!). The ring the beam draws on top is a gravitational-wave chirp, h+ and h×, its frequency rising as (tc − t)−3/8. Then a flash, silence, and the single dot the scope powered on with.

The name

It ends the way it started. The square wave was built from its Fourier series, and so is my name: spinning phasors chained tip to tail, the last tip writing RIVER DOWDY, getting sharper as more terms show up:

z(t) = Σn cn einωt

Under it my callsign goes out in Morse at 35 words per minute, and the speakers play the name's own Fourier sum, so that scope on your headphone jack reads it too.

Built with TypeScript, raw WebGPU, WGSL shaders and an AudioWorklet. No engine, no graphics libraries. The source is on GitHub. The lace behind this page is an Apollonian packing, the gasket Indra's pearls pack into.
