One beam

How the intro works, and why nothing in it is fake · River Dowdy

Ok so that minute before my projects? Up to the lip of the falls, every line on screen is drawn by one simulated oscilloscope beam. No video, no sprites, no images: a single dot of light flying around a phosphor screen, glowing wherever it has been and fading behind it. From the lip on it's rendered, but by the same rule.

There was exactly one rule: nothing on screen is fake. If something claims to be a signal, it is one. The equations up top are live, drawn by the same beam. And up to the falls, the sound is the picture: left speaker is the beam's x, right speaker is its y, thinned each moment to a table of points played at musical refresh rates as a bass line in A. So if you plugged a real scope into your headphone jack (please do this, it would make my week) it would draw the site.

The phosphor

A beam dragging across a tube dumps energy along its path. So every little step of the beam, from a to b, lays down a Gaussian spot smeared along that segment, which very kindly has a closed form, a difference of two error functions:

E(p) ∝ ∫01 e−|p − a − s(b−a)|² ⁄ 2σ² ds E ← E · e−Δt⁄τ

The screen holds on to that energy and lets it decay exponentially, the way real phosphor does. A few percent of it scatters around inside the glass as a faint halo that falls off as r−2.5 (this is the part that makes it look like an actual tube and not a line drawing). Brightness is energy per second, so it looks the same at 60 Hz and 120 Hz. And if your display can do HDR, the core of the trace is brighter than white, which is super fun.

A sine

It starts at power-on: the beam degausses, spiralling inward to a dot, and then settles into a pure 440 Hz sine, a circle on the scope swept out into a wave.

Then a copy of that wave steps up the harmonics and the sine takes its phase:

y = A cos(nωt + β sin ωt)

As the modulation index β swells to 2.405, the first zero of J0, the carrier vanishes, and a fan of FM waves fills in between. A reasonable person would stop here. I did not stop here.

The wave becomes a river

Each FM wave stops being a height and folds into a river. Rivers turn as they run, and the curve real meandering rivers follow is the sine-generated curve of Langbein and Leopold (yes, my name is River, yes this was always going to happen):

θ(s) = ω1 sin(2πs ⁄ M1) + ω2 sin(2πs ⁄ M2 + φ) z(s) = ∫ eiθ ds

Integrating eiθ means the river's direction is literally phase modulation. The fan of FM waves becomes a sheaf of rivers, which combs out onto the land's contour lines, and the valley carries those lines on.

The valley

The ground is a power-law random field, random phases under |k|−β, carved into a valley:

h(x) = 𝓕−1[ |k|−β eiφk ]

It's drawn the way the pulsar plot on Unknown Pleasures was drawn: cross-section after cross-section, each one hidden behind the ones in front of it, recomputed every frame. The firs are triangle waves under a tapering envelope. The water is streamlines of a real flow field, with a pool turning inside every bend.

Out to the speakers, the valley goes as its skyline, its hidden-line contour rows weighted by depth, and the outlines of the nearest firs. Then the camera flies down over the firs and runs for the lip.

The falls

The river ends in a round shaft and pours over its rim all the way round, by moonlight. The soundtrack's bands set how heavily each angle of the rim pours.

At the edge, the ink streamlines run into rendered water: a glassy sheet tearing into ropes and droplets, up to about a million droplets on the GPU, each placed as a pure function of its id and the time. A moonbow hangs at its true angles. From the lip on, the beam is done and the whole frame is rendered.

The whirlpool

If you've done any radio you have lost hours of your life staring at a waterfall plot: the spectrum of a signal, one row per moment, every new row shoving the old ones down the screen so the history pours away under you. Frequency across, time down, brightness is energy. It's called a waterfall because it looks like one. So at the bottom of the falls there's a real one.

The plunge pool turns as a Burgers vortex,

vθ(r) = (Γ ⁄ 2πr) (1 − e−r² ⁄ rc²)

and its surface is a real SDR waterfall plot of the soundtrack, computed live with a short-time Fourier transform:

X(m, ω) = Σn x[n] w[n − mH] e−iωn

Each ring is one row of the spectrum, the newest landing at the rim and the older rows winding inward, painted in an SDR display's colours: a speckled dark-blue noise floor through cyan and yellow to red for strong signals. The choir's notes form the bright spiral arms, while the camera rides one smooth path down onto the axis.

Near the drain, the water's light is read out as glyphs: every cell becomes the character whose ink best matches the light behind it, tinted by the plot's colours, spiralling into the drain.

The dive

The drain's throat flares into a cathedral nave, then a Menger sponge, a Mandelbox, and a coral foam, each found inside the last with their distance fields blended into a zoom without end.

Then a black hole. Every ray is bent by the Schwarzschild metric, traced with Binet's orbit equation for light:

u″ + u = 3Mu²

The rays cross a thin accretion disk whose colour includes relativistic beaming, Doppler shift, and gravitational redshift, and then the eye falls in.

The choir

The music of the fall is a Shepard tone: octaves sliding down forever under a fixed bell over log-frequency, so it always sounds like it's falling and it never gets anywhere:

x(t) = Σk A(log2 fk) sin φk(t) fk = 2k − D(t) f0

The drawing's own note before the falls is an A, and the choir grows out of it. It speeds up to three octaves a second at the horizon, where the whole choir is redshifted out of hearing, like a tape stopping (seen from outside, light from something falling in reddens with an e-folding time of 4M).

For a black hole of about twenty suns, like Cygnus X-1, the inner edge of the disk orbits 110 times a second, which is A2. A hot spot on it is Doppler-beamed once per orbit, so the low A you hear through the dark is the disk itself.

The arrival

Out of the dark (it was the inside of a thunderhead), we drop out of its base into a sunrise (ozone is what makes the sky overhead blue at sunrise).

We climb past a 47 km tall Rava Heart standing on its tip with its LEDs beating, then fly fast and low past every one of my projects, each name dropping into the index, and climb until the planet hangs whole.

The world

The planet is a small world where my projects stand. The hardware is the real thing: boards exported from KiCad, and mechanical CAD from Onshape and Fusion, baked to meshes and lit by a rasteriser plus a path tracer (the LEDs are real lamps the path tracer sees). Click a place and it shows the project working, then comes apart into the boards I designed; click a board and the camera goes to it.

The sky behind the planet is fractals baked into a panorama while the intro plays: Indra's pearls, a spiral galaxy with a Mandelbulb heart, a Kleinian cathedral, star streams along the Lorenz and Aizawa attractors, Julia-set filaments, a {7,3} hyperbolic tiling as an aurora, and an Apollonian ring nebula.

The music there is generative, keyed to the places nearby, and answers what they do: each keystroke PEEP types, each satellite Ephemeris 1 locks.

Built with TypeScript, raw WebGPU, WGSL shaders and an AudioWorklet. No engine, no graphics libraries. The source is on GitHub. The lace behind this page is an Apollonian packing, the same gasket as the ring nebula in the sky.
