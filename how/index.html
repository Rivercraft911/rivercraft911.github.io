One beam

How the intro works, and why every line of it is a signal · River Dowdy

The minute before my projects is drawn by a single simulated oscilloscope beam. There is no video in it, no sprites and no images. One spot of light moves across a phosphor screen a couple of million times a second, and wherever it has been, the screen glows and slowly fades. Everything you saw is wherever that spot went.

The rule I held myself to: nothing on screen is fake. If something claims to be a signal, it is one. The equation in the top corner is live, drawn by the same beam, and its numbers are the ones drawing the picture. And for the first half, the sound you hear is the picture: the left speaker is the beam's x and the right is its y, so a real oscilloscope plugged into the headphone jack would draw the site.

The phosphor

A beam moving across a tube lays down energy along its path, so each step of the beam deposits a Gaussian spot integrated along the segment from a to b, which comes out in closed form as a difference of error functions:

E(p) ∝ ∫01 e−|p − a − s(b−a)|² ⁄ 2σ² ds    E ← E · e−Δt⁄τ

The screen keeps that energy and lets it decay exponentially, the way phosphor does. A few percent of it scatters in the glass as a faint halo that falls off as r−2.5. Brightness is energy per second, so it looks the same at 60 Hz and 120 Hz, and on displays that can show it, the core of the trace is brighter than white.

A square wave

It opens on the oldest trick in signal processing: a square wave is a sum of sines, and you can watch it assemble.

x(t) = (4 ⁄ π) Σk sin((2k+1)ωt) ⁄ (2k+1)

Each harmonic arrives like a control loop locking: it overshoots, rings and settles. Almost all the motion in the intro moves this way, as a sum of second-order step responses, which keeps every frame a pure function of time (you can scrub it):

s(τ) = 1 − e−ζωnτ (cos ωdτ + (ζωn ⁄ ωd) sin ωdτ)

That is why the corners flicker as they sharpen. Those are the Gibbs ears, and you can hear each harmonic come in.

So far, so Signals and Systems 101. Every course starts here, and a reasonable person would stop here too…

The wave becomes a river

Then the square wave stops being a height and becomes a heading. A river turns as it runs, and the curve real meandering rivers follow is the sine-generated curve of Langbein and Leopold:

θ(s) = ω sin(2πs ⁄ M)

Fed the square wave, θ makes a zigzag; as its harmonics let go, the elbows round off into meanders. The river's direction eiθ is phase modulation, so as the bends swell you can hear Bessel sidebands bloom. The same Bessel function sets how winding the river is, the length along the channel over the distance down the valley:

sinuosity = 1 ⁄ J0(ω)

That line lands exactly on the river in the valley.

The valley

The ground is a power-law random field, random phases under |k|−β, the same spectral trick behind most synthetic terrain, carved into a valley:

h(x) = 𝓕−1[ |k|−β eiφk ]

It is drawn the way the famous pulsar plot on Unknown Pleasures was drawn: cross-section after cross-section, each hidden behind the ones in front of it, recomputed every frame. The firs are triangle waves under a tapering envelope. The water is streamlines of a flow field. The stones in the shallows are a 16APSK constellation, the kind a satellite modem uses.

The waterfall

Every radio engineer has spent hours staring at a waterfall plot: the spectrum of a signal, one row per moment, each new row pushing the old ones down the screen so the history pours away beneath you. Frequency runs across, time runs down, brightness is energy. It is called a waterfall because it looks like one. So at the end of the river there is a real one.

The camera flies to the lip of the falls, and the falling water stops pretending. Each lane of the curtain is a frequency bin. The lip is the live spectrum of exactly what you are hearing, and it tips over the edge row after row, so the curtain below is the recent past of the soundtrack, falling. It is the short-time Fourier transform of the audio, computed as it plays:

X(m, ω) = Σn x[n] w[n − mH] e−iωn

The camera turns to face the falls, and then it goes over with the water.

The fall

From the fall on, the tube becomes a raster CRT too: full-screen fields, rendered on the GPU, swept into the same phosphor under the beam. The falls land in a plunge pool that is already turning, a Burgers vortex,

vθ(r) = (Γ ⁄ 2πr) (1 − e−r² ⁄ rc²)

and the pool is still the waterfall plot, wound into a circle: angle is log-frequency and log-radius is age, so the water carries the spectrum's history inward. The soundtrack's octaves become its spiral arms.

Text then spreads over the whirlpool like an infection. Every cell becomes the teleprinter glyph whose ink best matches the light behind it, and the centre row is transmitted as real RTTY: 45.45 baud, Baudot, 170 Hz shift. You could decode it.

Down the drain is Indra's net: the limit set of a Kleinian group (Maskit's slice, from Indra's Pearls), generated by just two Möbius maps,

a(z) = μ + 1 ⁄ z     b(z) = z + 2

with every pearl a soap bubble lit by thin-film interference. On the floor the beam plays the chaos game, applying random words in a, b and their inverses to a point, so the dust it leaves is the same limit set.

The multiverse

Then come cuts through ten worlds, accelerating: a kaleidoscopic IFS, the Mandelbulb, an Apollonian packing, a Lyapunov fractal grown from the DVB-S2 start-of-frame word, Newton's method run on the roots of a 32APSK constellation, the {4,3,5} hyperbolic honeycomb and others. Every world keeps running while you are elsewhere, and every world bends the music its own way. Last is a quaternion Julia set, a four-dimensional fractal, sliced by a plane turning through 4D.

The choir

The music under the fall is a Shepard tone: octaves sliding down forever under a fixed bell-shaped loudness, so it always seems to fall and never arrives:

x(t) = Σk A(log₂ fk) sin φk(t),    fk = 2k − D(t) f0

where D(t) is how many octaves we have fallen and A is a fixed bell over log-frequency. Its partials are pure octaves from 40 Hz to 5 kHz, played in a small feedback-delay-network hall. The visual rule is the same: each world is an endless zoom, and the zoom rate is the choir's rate of descent. You are falling as fast as you hear yourself falling.

The black hole

At the end, the Julia set falls into a black hole set in a pseudo-Kleinian cathedral. The light rays are bent by the Schwarzschild metric on their way through the scene, so its arches wrap into Einstein rings around the shadow:

x″ = −3M h² x ⁄ r⁵

Then the cathedral itself is torn in, spaghettified and wound into a maelstrom by the shear of its orbits. We fall with angular momentum 3.5M, just short of the 4M that would have let us escape, so we spiral in. The ring the beam draws over it is a gravitational-wave chirp, h+ and h×, its frequency rising as (tc − t)−3/8. Then a flash, silence, and the single dot the oscilloscope powered on with.

The name

It ends the way it began. The square wave was built from its Fourier series; my name is too, spinning phasors chained tip to tail whose last tip writes RIVER DOWDY, sharpening as terms arrive:

z(t) = Σn cn einωt

Under it my callsign goes out in Morse at 35 words per minute, and the speakers play the name's own Fourier sum, so that scope on the headphone jack reads it too.

Built with TypeScript, raw WebGPU and WGSL shaders, and an AudioWorklet: no engine and no graphics libraries. The source is on GitHub. The lace behind this page is an Apollonian packing, the gasket Indra's pearls pack into.
